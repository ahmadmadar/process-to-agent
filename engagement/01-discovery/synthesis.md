# Discovery Synthesis: Claims Process (draft for consultant review)

> Fictional engagement for portfolio purposes. The client, stakeholders and data are synthetic.

**Sources:** T1 (COO), T2 (claims lead), IT data release 1 (`data/released/`).
Every figure cites a query in `analysis/results.md` (generated by
`analysis/data_checks.py`). Claude drafted this; the rulings in §4 are the
consultant's to make.

---

## 1. Bottom line

1. **Claims are slow because they wait, mostly after the decision.** The
   longest measured stage is decision to finance pre-approval (Q4). The
   elapsed time from complete documents to a decision is short (Q4), and the
   stated hands-on effort is about three hours (T2:L66). The COO's view that staff are the bottleneck (T1:L24) is not
   supported.
2. **Finance pre-approval is the leading candidate to eliminate.** It touches
   almost every claim, including denials, and almost never changes one (Q5),
   matching the claims lead's account (T2:L56). It is not "a day or two"
   (T1:L26; Q5).
3. **"How much we recover" has no agreed definition.** The two stated figures
   (T1:L18, T2:L64) each match a different denominator in the data (Q9).
4. **Account-manager payments are an unresolved ownership conflict with a
   measurable cost.** Claims they decide are paid before documents are
   complete far more often, filed with carriers less often, and recover far
   less (Q11). The COO endorses the practice (T1:L10); the claims lead opposes
   it (T2:L14, T2:L78).
5. **The COO's autonomy line conflicts with the insurer's rules.** Auto-paying
   up to $50k (T1:L12) would cover claims above the assumed deductible (Q7),
   where the insurer must be notified and consent before settlement (T2:L16).
   The one account-manager payment made before notice lost cover (Q12).

## 2. Findings

### F1. Delay is waiting, concentrated after the decision (H1)

- **Stated:** "a month, sometimes more" (T1:L22); "four weeks, give or take",
  "most of that is waiting" (T2:L12). COO: most of it is hands-on work
  (T1:L24). Claims lead: about three hours of work per claim (T2:L66), waiting
  on customer documents and finance (T2:L26).
- **Data:** cycle-time median and p90 are in Q3; both stated figures are
  close to the median. Stage medians (Q4): received → docs complete and
  docs complete → decision are short; decision → finance pre-approval is the
  longest stage; pre-approval → paid adds the wait for the payment run.
  Every payment falls on a Thursday (Q6), matching the weekly run (T2:L18).
- **Limit:** the event log has one date per step (README), so working and
  waiting time inside a stage cannot be separated. The three-hour estimate is
  untimed (T2:L66).

### F2. Finance pre-approval almost never changes the outcome (H2)

- **Stated:** every claim, any amount, any outcome, including denial letters,
  goes to the AP analyst (T2:L18, T2:L60). Started in 2019 after one fraud
  case; changes are rare and "always been a big one" (T2:L56). The claims team
  does not know what it checks (T2:L54). COO: "a quick check… a day or two at
  most, a sensible control" (T1:L26).
- **Data:** nearly all reviewed claims are approved as submitted; the
  exceptions are listed in Q5 and are both large claims. Denials go through
  the same step (Q5). Median and p90 decision → pre-approval days are in Q5.
- **Implication:** this is the strongest "eliminate, don't automate" candidate
  found so far. Whether to remove it, keep it above a threshold, or keep it is
  the consultant's call, and the control owner (CFO, per T1:L30 and T2:L68)
  has not been interviewed.

### F3. Recovery rate depends on what you divide by (H5)

- **Stated:** COO "roughly 70 percent back", "basically fine" (T1:L18); claims
  lead "maybe a third of what we pay out… less than it should be" (T2:L12),
  "my sense of it rather than a report" (T2:L64).
- **Data (Q9):** recovered ÷ paid across all paid claims is close to the
  claims lead's figure. Recovered ÷ paid on claims the carrier accepted is
  close to the COO's figure. The COO's number is consistent with an
  accepted-claims rate, but its source is unknown (T1:L20); confirm with the
  CFO.
- **Also (Q9):** a large share of paid claims is never filed with a carrier,
  and some filed claims are still pending, so the all-claims rate will move as
  those resolve.

### F4. Account-manager payments bypass the process and cost recovery (H4, H5)

- **Stated:** AMs "pay their key customers early… before we've got photos or a
  delivery receipt, and then I've got nothing to file" (T2:L14); the team finds
  out "after the money's gone" (T2:L54). COO: "that's how it should be… a
  commercial decision" (T1:L10).
- **Data:** AMs decide claims on both sides of the $10k line (Q8), so the
  approval line (T2:L14) does not bind them. Compared with the claims team,
  AM-decided claims are paid before docs are complete far more often, filed
  with the carrier less often, and recover a small fraction of what the team
  recovers (Q11). AM-decided claims make up all of the approvals without
  complete documents (Q13).

### F5. Insurer notice and consent constrain large claims

- **Stated:** claims over the deductible ("twenty-five, I think?") must be
  reported within 30 days, and the insurer must consent before settlement
  (T2:L16, T2:L48). A key-account claim paid early by an AM lost cover; "two
  other big ones" had late notice and the insurer reserved its rights (T2:L16).
- **Data (Q12):** every claim with an insurer-notified step is above the
  assumed deductible, and every claim above it has one. One claim was paid
  before notice, decided by an AM, notified after 30 days, and cover was
  declined, consistent with T2:L16. One other was notified after 30 days and
  drew a reservation of rights. The data shows one such case, not two; check
  with the claims lead or CFO.
- **Implication:** the COO's $50k autonomy line (T1:L12, T1:L34) includes
  claims above the assumed deductible (Q7). The deductible itself is an
  assumption until the CFO confirms it.

### F6. Rules are unwritten and partly invisible (H3)

- **Stated:** no written policy; approval levels live in an old email
  (T2:L14, T2:L36); rules are learned on the job (T2:L36, T2:L46). Fallback for
  missing documents is "judgment" (T2:L34). COO cannot describe the detailed
  rules (T1:L28).
- **Data (Q14):** denial and partial reasons include "outside customer filing
  window" and "customer packaging". Neither interviewee mentioned a filing
  window or a packaging rule; both are rules to define in requirements.
- **Rule to test:** clean-POD shortages are never filed because carriers
  "deny every single time" (T2:L20, T2:L64). Release 1 has no POD-exception
  field, so this cannot be tested (Q10). Q10 does show filed shortage claims
  being paid by carriers, but not whether any of them had a clean POD.

### F7. Temperature claims are a time-critical edge case the data cannot see

- **Stated:** reefer temperature downloads are kept about 72 hours, must be
  requested by phone the same day, and three customers' contracts make
  Northline liable regardless of the carrier (T2:L44). Not written down
  (T2:L46); the claims lead wants them flagged to a person immediately
  (T2:L58).
- **Data:** the only trace is a missing `temp_log` on some claims (Q13). There
  is no reefer flag, download date or cold-chain customer flag in release 1.

### F8. Volume, mix and claim size match what both stakeholders said

- Volume per month including excluded loss claims (Q1) matches "about 70
  claims a month" (T1:L18). The claims lead's mix (T2:L50) matches Q2.
- Median claim size and the count over $25k in the six months (Q7) match "a
  couple of thousand" and "a dozen or so a half-year" (T2:L48).

## 4. Contradictions for the consultant's ruling

Claude does not rule on these. Each needs a ruling logged in
`docs/consultant-decisions.md`.

1. **Where the time goes:** work (T1:L24) vs waiting (T2:L26). Data: Q4.
2. **Finance pre-approval:** quick, sensible control (T1:L26) vs slow, rarely
   changes anything (T2:L26, T2:L56). Data: Q5.
3. **Recovery rate:** ~70% (T1:L18) vs ~⅓ (T2:L64). Data: Q9 (definition).
4. **Who owns payouts:** AMs for key accounts (T1:L10) vs the claims team
   (T2:L14, T2:L78). Data: Q8, Q11. Sponsor ratification needed (SOW §8).
5. **Agent autonomy:** auto-pay and deny under $50k (T1:L12, T1:L34) vs
   denials, insurer-touching and temperature claims always to a person
   (T2:L16, T2:L58). Data: Q7, Q12.
6. **Disagreements:** none come to mind (T1:L32) vs a declined insurer claim
   that is "still a sore point" (T2:L16). Data: Q12.
